# Education interface review — November 17, 2026

This review covers the student and single-student teacher experience. The current
browser tool failed during initialization, so this is a source, interaction and
design review, not a claim that the final screens were visually inspected.

## Product comparisons

The following patterns were checked against the products' official pages. They
inform the implementation; their characters, artwork and branding are not reused.

| Product | Useful pattern | Application here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mathigon](https://mathigon.org/teachers) | Mathematical manipulatives and separate lesson/task resources support exploration. | Use number lines, fraction models and geometry in lesson content; put teaching materials beside the dated lesson. Decorative mathematics should not obscure the problem. |
| [Khan Academy Kids](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049358751-Learn-more-about-the-characters-inside-Khan-Academy-Kids) | Characters guide the learning experience and give feedback about what the learner did. | Original geometry characters point to the next action and briefly celebrate submitted work. Keep the reading surface still. Adopt the guidance pattern without adopting the younger app's tone. |
| [Khan Academy Kids Learning Path](https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048828572-Learn-more-about-the-Learning-Path) | A guided sequence provides a clear place to start. | Today has one primary start/continue action. Optional skill browsing remains secondary. |
| [Prodigy goals and rewards](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/teachers/rewards) | Teachers manage goals and rewards together. | Put the rule, numerical example, next point, prize requests and ledger on one page. A single learner does not need classroom leaderboards or multiple reward currencies. |
| [DragonBox Geometry](https://www.dragonbox.com/products/elements) | Character progression is connected to the mathematical activity and shape properties. | Use a coherent geometry-character family and topic motifs. Do not introduce unrelated mini-games between every question. |
| [Brilliant learning paths](https://brilliant.org/help/features/what-are-learning-paths/) | Guided sequences combine explanations with practice. | A question's help belongs beside that question; the student should be able to move from a hint to a worked step or a short tutor explanation without navigating away. |

These are design inferences from the product descriptions, not comparative
evidence that one product produces better outcomes.

## Information architecture

Teacher navigation should have four primary destinations and Settings:

- **Overview:** current lesson, daily/catch-up progress, note and three teaching actions.
- **Curriculum & dates:** ten weeks, daily topics, worksheets, teacher guide,
  week download, exact-date editing and a preview before applying changes.
  Practice tasks and question samples are secondary tabs within this area.
- **Student work:** answers, skills, help history, mistake patterns and paper checks.
- **Points & rewards:** active rules, next-point example, rule editing, prizes,
  requests and ledger. Point adjustments are secondary controls.
- **Settings:** accounts, passwords, helper limits and backups.

The overview should not display every editing form. Its progress-report textarea
belongs in a clearly named disclosure. Account creation is not a primary action
for an established one-student classroom.

Student navigation should retain Daily questions as the default starting point.
The catch-up action becomes the recommended next action once today's goal is met.
The reward card explains progress and links to the prize page; it should not
compete with the daily button as another primary start action.

## Accounting language

The interface must distinguish three quantities without implying three unrelated
question banks:

1. **Today:** the first daily-target answers satisfy today's required goal.
2. **Earlier work:** later answers reduce the oldest already-due deficit.
3. **Points:** all saved answers today contribute to the active point threshold.
   A catch-up answer can both reduce overdue work and earn a point.

With the default rules, the first point is at 35 total answers today and later
points are at 45, 55, and so on. Points remain saved; the daily threshold resets.
Wrong answers and using help do not subtract points. The primary daily goal meter
should stop at 20/20; actual activity can separately say 25 answers today.

## Concrete issues found during implementation

- Repeated student-list and serial progress-summary requests delayed teacher
  navigation. The page should load its own content without waiting for a global
  report on every route.
- The old points form lived in Settings while prize management lived elsewhere.
  Move the authoritative rules form into Points & rewards.
- “Choose extra practice” was misleading for a teacher task inserted into the
  daily set. Name its effect on daily focus and workload explicitly.
- “Move back one week” was ambiguous. Use earlier/later labels, a date input and
  a preview naming all affected weeks.
- Moving a week to a non-Saturday exposed hardcoded Saturday-first labels in
  student copy and calendar headings. Derive weekdays from assigned dates and
  show gaps between shifted weeks accurately.
- A large backlog banner, next-point action, mixed practice and review action
  competed with the daily task. Keep totals visible but translate them into a
  short next task.
- The old `.teacher-backlog-hero` CSS was intended for a full-width hero. It must
  be replaced when the element becomes one column inside the progress board.
- SPA navigation needs immediate loading feedback and a focus move to the new
  main heading after explicit navigation. Answer feedback must not cause the
  same page-level focus jump.

## Interaction and visual acceptance criteria

- A learner can identify the next action without reading a long explanation.
- The teacher can reach the current week's packet, move its date, or change the
  points rules from clearly named destinations.
- A date change is previewed, can be cancelled, rejects overlapping weeks, and
  preserves submitted answers and points. Packet notes distinguish original
  printed dates from the student's assigned dates.
- Action labels describe effects. Meaning does not depend only on color or icon.
- Primary controls target at least 44 pixels in height; focus is visible;
  disclosure summaries have usable click areas.
- Progress meters expose bounded numeric values and descriptive labels.
- Optional animations finish quickly, do not cover questions or delay Next,
  and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Blue, violet, yellow and coral accents distinguish learning actions while
  body text stays dark and readable. Mathematical diagrams retain their labels.
- Narrow layouts stack sections and wrap controls; tables scroll within their
  own region rather than widening the entire page.

## Educational and accessibility references

- [IES: Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/1)
  supports spaced study, worked examples alongside problems, and explanatory
  graphics with words.
- [CAST: Meaning and purpose of goals](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/meaning-purpose-goals/)
  informs clear daily goals and manageable catch-up steps.
- [CAST: Action-oriented feedback](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/feedback/)
  informs specific next-step feedback rather than vague praise.
- [W3C: Reduced motion technique](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39.html)
  and [target size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)
  inform motion preferences and usable controls.
